# BOATSWAIN: the tutorial plan

A plan for teaching the FM-1 and SLOOP 3 from zero, on the FM-1 itself. Nothing here is built yet.
Draft 2, 2026-11-16.

**One course, two ways to take it:** led by Claude, or from a web page with no AI. The Claude-led way is
built first. The web page is how most FM-1 owners will take the course, so it is planned from the start and
never treated as an extra (section 5).

## 1. The goal

**A 10-year-old beginner finishes the course able to make her own songs on the FM-1, with the tutorial
switched off.**

That means she can:
- make a drum beat from an empty track;
- add a bass line, chords and a melody on the other tracks;
- pick and shape sounds;
- use the effects, both the ones that stay on and the ones you play;
- build several patterns and join them into a song with a beginning, a middle and an end;
- save her work and fix her own mistakes.

**The final test is simple:** an empty project, no lit buttons, no hints. She makes a whole song and saves it.

The author plays every lesson first, as a real beginner with a new FM-1. What goes wrong for him is fixed
before a child sees it.

## 2. What a lesson feels like

Here is lesson 4, "Sixteen boxes", as she would live it. It takes about eight minutes.

1. **The screen says what the idea is,** in a few short lines:

   ```
    SIXTEEN BOXES    1/7
   --------------------
    A SEQUENCER IS A
    ROW OF 16 BOXES.
    A LIGHT WALKS
    ALONG THEM.

    OCT- AGAIN   GO OCT+
   ```

2. **The FM-1 shows it.** A light walks along the 16 white keys in time with a bass line that is already
   playing. She hears nothing new yet: the boxes are empty.
3. **One button blinks: SEQ.** The screen says `PRESS SEQ`. She presses it, and SLOOP's real drum grid opens.
   A strip across the screen keeps the instruction in view.
4. **White key 1 blinks.** `PUT A KICK IN BOX 1`. She presses it. The next time the light walks past, the
   kick plays.
5. **Keys 5, 9 and 13 are named but not lit.** She finds them herself. If she waits too long, they start to
   glow.
6. **The band joins in.** The bass gets a melody and chords on top of her kick. The screen tells her what
   she has made: `THIS IS A HOUSE BEAT`.
7. **Free play.** `NOW CHANGE IT. MOVE A KICK.` Nothing she does here is wrong.
8. **A question, answered with the two octave buttons,** which both blink:

   ```
    WHICH BOX IS THE
    FIRST BEAT OF
    THE BAR?

    OCT- 4       1 OCT+
   ```

9. **The boatswain's call.** Three keys light one after the other. She plays them back, and the next lesson
   opens.

Every press in steps 3 to 7 is the real SLOOP press. There is no pretend mode, so what she learns still
works when the tutorial is off.

**With Claude leading, the lesson bends to her.** Say she puts the kicks in boxes 5, 9 and 12. Claude sees
that and answers on the screen: `CLOSE. ONE KICK IS EARLY. LISTEN.` It plays her bar, then lets box 13 glow.
In free play, if she makes a beat of her own, Claude tells her something true about that beat.

**From the web page, the same lesson follows its written path.** The three boxes glow after a wait, the
check still catches the early kick, and a written hint appears. Free play gets no comment.

## 3. The rules of teaching

- **One idea per lesson.** Five to ten minutes. Thirty short lessons beat ten long ones.
- **Hear it, do it, change it, own it.** Every lesson has those four parts, in that order:
  - the FM-1 plays or shows the idea;
  - she does it with the lights guiding her;
  - she changes it freely;
  - she does it again from memory in a later lesson.
- **The real instrument.** She presses the same buttons SLOOP uses, on SLOOP's own screens.
- **The lights fade.** Help comes in three levels, and each skill moves down them:
  1. the button blinks and the screen names it;
  2. the screen names it, no light;
  3. the screen gives only the goal (`ADD A SNARE ON 5 AND 13`).
- **Every lesson ends in music.** Her part sits inside a full song, never alone.
- **Nothing can be broken.** Undo comes in the first rank. Her own song is put away safely before a lesson
  starts and put back after. Any lesson can be started again.
- **The same picture every time.** A step is always a "box", a pattern is always a "page" (section 6).
- **Few words on the screen.** The FM-1's screen fits about eight lines of twenty capital letters. That limit
  suits a ten-year-old: the FM-1 shows, and the words only point.
- **A review to open each lesson.** Thirty seconds, no lights, on something from two or three lessons back.

## 4. The tutorial's own controls

These are the only things she has to learn about the tutorial itself. Lesson 1 teaches all of them.

| Control | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A blinking button or key** | Press this now. |
| **A faint key** | Part of the picture, such as the boxes that already hold a drum. |
| **The card** | A full screen of words, for an idea or a question. |
| **The strip** | One or two lines over SLOOP's own screen, while she works. |
| **OCT− and OCT+, both blinking** | A question with two answers. The bottom line of the screen puts one answer by each button. While a question is up, they don't change the octave. |
| **Both OCT buttons together** | "I'm stuck." She gets a hint, then the choices below. |
| **The boatswain's call** | A short tune that ends a lesson. Keys light one at a time and she plays them back. |

**"Do it for me" comes in three sizes.** She can ask at any point, and none of them counts against her:
- **SHOW ME:** the FM-1 does the next move itself, slowly, with the button lit as it goes. Then it undoes
  the move and she tries.
- **FINISH IT:** the rest of the part she is working on is written in, for example the hi-hats.
- **BRING THE BAND:** the other tracks are added, so her part plays inside a finished song.

**The boatswain's call.** On a real ship the boatswain gives orders with a whistle, a few notes for each
order. Here the call means "carry on". Each rank has its own call of three to five notes. Put end to end,
the four calls are the course's theme tune, so by the last lesson she can play it all.

**A wrong press never loses anything.** The screen says `NOT THAT ONE` and the right button blinks. If she
wanders to another screen, the strip tells her how to come back.

## 5. One course, two ways to take it

The same lessons, the same songs and the same firmware serve both.

| | Led by Claude | From a web page, no AI |
|---|---|---|
| **Who it is for** | Anyone with Claude. Built first. | Every FM-1 owner. Likely most of the community. |
| **Who leads** | Claude, watching each try and choosing what comes next. | The web page, following the lesson's standard path. |
| **Lit buttons, cards, questions, the call** | Yes | Yes |
| **Checks her work** | Yes | Yes |
| **Do it for me, all three sizes** | Yes, from the song as written. | Yes, from the song as written. |
| **Hints** | Made for what she just did. | The lesson's written hints, in order. |
| **Pace** | Skips ahead, or explains another way. | Set turns: a hint after a wait, practice skipped when she answers `TOO EASY`. |
| **Talks about her own music** | Yes | No |
| **Answers her questions** | Yes | No |

### How Claude leads

- **The lesson file is Claude's teaching notes.** It holds the idea, the song, the standard path, the
  written hints, and what "she's got it" looks like.
- **Claude sends one move at a time.** A move is a small bundle: show these words, blink SEQ, then key 1,
  and report what she pressed. The FM-1 follows a move at once, so she never waits on a single press.
- **Then Claude looks and decides.** About every half minute it sees what she did and picks the next move,
  the way a teacher watches a try and then responds.
- **The wait is covered.** Claude needs a few seconds to think. The loop keeps playing, and the screen shows
  a small "thinking" sign.
- **Claude teaches, it doesn't compose.** During a lesson all the music comes from the song as written and
  approved. Claude changes only the words, the lights and the pace. That keeps a lesson simple and the
  music good every time.

**What Claude adds that no manual, video or fixed lesson can:**
- **It sees what she made.** It reads her pattern from the FM-1 and talks about her beat, not a sample beat:
  "your snare is in boxes 4 and 12, try 5 and 13 and hear the difference".
- **It answers her questions,** typed or spoken at the computer, about the thing in front of her.

### Four rules that keep the web page version strong

1. **Every lesson has a complete standard path in its file:** every word, light, check and hint. Nothing a
   learner needs lives only in Claude's head.
2. **Claude starts from the standard path** and leaves it only when the learner needs something else.
3. **What works goes back into the file.** When Claude explains a step another way and it lands, that
   becomes a written hint. Every Claude-led session makes the web page version better.
4. **The standard path is tested with no AI,** on the simulated FM-1 (section 9). The web page version is
   known to work before the web page exists.

### The parts

| | What it is | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| **The FM-1** | BOATSWAIN: SLOOP 3 plus one small addition, the guide | Shows the words, lights the buttons, and reports every press. |
| **The connector** | A small program on the computer, joined to the FM-1 by the USB cable | Claude's hands. It passes each move to the FM-1 and brings back what happened. |
| **The web page** | A page for Chrome or Edge, like SLOOP's editor | Plays each lesson's standard path over the same cable, with nothing to install. |
| **The lesson files** | Plain text in this repository | Read by Claude and by the web page alike. |

**A sketch of lesson 4,** to show the idea (the exact form gets settled when the first lesson is built):

```
lesson:  4  SIXTEEN BOXES
idea:    a sequencer is a row of 16 boxes and a walking light
song:    house-one     she plays: the kick     band: bass, chords, melody
got it:  kick in boxes 1 5 9 13, and she knows which box starts the bar

standard path
  card:   A SEQUENCER IS A ROW OF 16 BOXES. A LIGHT WALKS ALONG THEM.
  show:   walk a light along the 16 white keys, in time
  do:     open the drum grid        light: SEQ
  do:     kick in box 1             light: key 1
  do:     kick in boxes 5 9 13      help: named, not lit
  check:  kick on 1 5 9 13
  band:   bass, chords, melody
  ask:    WHICH BOX IS THE FIRST BEAT OF THE BAR?   OCT-: 4   OCT+: 1 (right)
  call:   greenhand

hints
  kick in boxes 5 9 13:   COUNT FOUR BOXES ALONG FROM YOUR KICK.
  then:                   let the three keys glow
```

## 6. Explaining the big ideas

Each idea gets one picture, used the same way in every lesson, and one way the FM-1 shows it.

| Word | What we say | How the FM-1 shows it |
|---|---|---|
| **Groovebox** | A band in a box: the players, a notebook that remembers what each one plays, and a mixing desk. | Mute the five tracks one by one while a song plays. |
| **Track** | One player in the band. There are five: four instruments and a drummer. | Each has its own colour on the screen. |
| **Step** | A box. A bar has 16 boxes. | The 16 white keys are the 16 boxes. |
| **Sequencer** | The notebook. A light walks along the boxes, and a full box plays. | The walking light on the keys. |
| **Tempo** | How fast the light walks. | Turn SELECT and watch it speed up. |
| **Loop** | When the light reaches the end, it starts again. | It simply never stops. |
| **Pattern** | A page of the notebook. Each player has eight pages. | Hold SAVE: eight keys light, one per page. |
| **Song** | The order the pages are played in. | The SONG screen, line by line. |
| **Sound** | Which instrument a player holds. | Turn PRESETS while a part plays. |
| **Effect** | A room or a costume for the sound: an echo, a fuzz, a big hall. | Hold FX and press a key. |
| **Mix** | How loud each player is. | Hold GLO and turn the four knobs. |

## 7. The course

Four ranks, thirty lessons. A greenhand is a sailor on a first voyage. Each rank ends with a **voyage**: a
piece she makes herself, with no lit buttons.

The button moves below come from SLOOP's guides. Each one gets checked on the simulated FM-1 when its lesson
is built, because SLOOP 3 is still changing.

### Rank 1, GREENHAND: your first beat

| # | Lesson | The one idea | What she does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcome aboard | A blinking light means "press me". | Turns MASTER, starts and stops a song with PLAY, answers a question with the OCT buttons, plays the first call. |
| 2 | Meet the crew | A groovebox is a band in a box. | With a song playing, holds GLO and taps keys 1 to 5 to silence each player, then brings them back. |
| 3 | The drum kit | The drummer's 16 sounds are on the white keys. | Turns ALGORITHM to the drum track, finds the kick, snare and hat, plays along, tries other kits with PRESETS. |
| 4 | Sixteen boxes | A sequencer is a row of boxes and a walking light. | Opens the drum grid with SEQ and puts a kick in boxes 1, 5, 9 and 13. |
| 5 | Kick, snare, hat | A beat is three simple layers. | Adds the snare on 5 and 13 and the hats, picking each sound with a black key. |
| 6 | Faster, slower, oops | Tempo is how fast the light walks. Undo takes a mistake back. | Changes the tempo with SELECT, makes a mess on purpose, undoes it (EDIT + OCT−), saves (SAVE twice). |
| 7 | **First voyage** | | Makes her own beat on an empty drum track. Claude says what it hears, and the band joins in. |

### Rank 2, DECKHAND: add the band

| # | Lesson | The one idea | What she does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | A bass line in boxes | The other players put notes in boxes, and low notes are the bass. | On track 1, plays a key, then holds SEQ and taps a box to drop the note in. Four notes under her beat. |
| 9 | Pick your sound | Each player can hold any instrument. | Turns PRESETS, opens SOUNDS, picks by category, keeps a favourite. |
| 10 | Record it live | REC writes down what you play. | Presses REC and plays over the loop. Undoes a bad take and tries again. |
| 11 | No wrong notes | A key is a family of notes that sound good together. | Sets the key (SEL + a key), then plays a tune on the white keys by following the lights. |
| 12 | One-finger chords | A chord is several notes at once. | Turns chords on (SEL + KNOB 1) and records four chords on track 3. |
| 13 | The piano roll | Notes can be moved, stretched and removed. | Opens NOTES (SEQ twice), fixes a wrong note, makes one longer. |
| 14 | Mixing the crew | If everyone shouts, nobody is heard. | Sets the five levels (GLO + the knobs), uses mute and solo to listen to one player. |
| 15 | **Second voyage** | | A four-track loop: drums, bass, chords, melody. She makes at least two of the parts and may ask for the rest. |

### Rank 3, ABLE HAND: make it move

| # | Lesson | The one idea | What she does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | Eight pages | A pattern is a page, and each player has eight. | Holds SAVE, starts page 2, copies page 1 onto it and changes it: a verse beat and a chorus beat. |
| 17 | Fills and rolls | A fill says "something new is coming". | Plays a fill by hand (GLO + a key), writes one that only plays as a fill, makes a hat roll (ARP + a key). |
| 18 | Play the effects | Some effects are played like notes. | Holds FX and presses keys for the punch-in effects, then locks them on (FX + HOME) and plays them with both hands. |
| 19 | Echo and space | Delay repeats a sound. Reverb puts it in a room. | Sends the melody to the delay and the snare to the reverb from the FX pages. |
| 20 | Colour a track | An insert changes one player. A filter makes it dark or bright. | Puts FUZZ or CRUSH on the bass, sweeps the filter on the whole mix (FX + KNOB 1). |
| 21 | Shape a sound | A sound has a beginning and an end. | On the ENV and EDIT pages, makes one sound start slowly, ring longer, get brighter. |
| 22 | Wobble and arps | The FM-1 can move a sound, or play the notes of a chord, by itself. | Adds a wobble with the LFO page, then holds a chord and lets the arpeggiator play it. |
| 23 | **Third voyage** | | A piece with two pages, a fill and effects, played live. |

### Rank 4, BOATSWAIN: make a song

| # | Lesson | The one idea | What she does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | Songs have parts | Intro, verse, chorus, ending. | Listens to a full song and names each part as it comes, with the OCT buttons. |
| 25 | Chain the pages | Pages played in a row make a song. | Holds SAVE and taps pages in order, then records a song live (SONG REC). |
| 26 | Write it line by line | A song can be written down and edited. | Opens SONG 2/2 and sets each line: which page, how many bars. |
| 27 | Build-ups and drops | Taking players away is as strong as adding them. | Arranges with mutes, fills and filter sweeps: a quiet intro, a build, a drop. |
| 28 | Swing and feel | The same beat can march or dance. | Adds swing, and soft and hard hits (OCT− and OCT+ while drumming). |
| 29 | Keep it and share it | A finished song deserves to be kept. | Saves the project, puts a copy in the sea chest on the computer, records the song as a sound file to send to family. |
| 30 | **The boatswain's test** | | An empty project and no lights. A whole song: three parts or more, four tracks or more, effects, saved. Claude helps only when asked. |

**Shore leave: extra lessons, in any order, once a rank is done.** The visualisers, colour themes, her own
drum sounds and breakbeats (these need SLOOP's web editor), the DX7 sounds, and playing the FM-1 from
another keyboard.

## 8. The songs

**Every lesson builds toward a song, and a song lasts several lessons.** She puts the kick into the house
song in lesson 4 and its snare and hats in lesson 5. The song grows as she does.

**All the music is original, written for the course in the styles a groovebox is made for.** No old tunes
with a beat put under them. A style belongs to everyone: the house kick, the hip-hop backbeat, a four-chord
loop. Only a particular song belongs to someone, so the course borrows the feel of a style and never a song.

**Each skill is taught in the style that is built on it.** The lesson then explains two things at once: how
the FM-1 works, and how that kind of music works.

| Style | What it teaches | Lessons |
|---|---|---|
| **House** | A kick in boxes 1, 5, 9 and 13 is the house beat. | 4, 5 |
| **Hip-hop** | A steady snare, and a kick that moves around it. | 5 to 7 |
| **Funk and disco** | A bass line that dances with the kick. | 8, 10 |
| **Pop** | Four chords in a loop, and a tune over them. | 11, 12 |
| **Lo-fi** | Soft chords, mellow sounds, and a gentle mix. | 9, 13, 14 |
| **Trap** | Fast hat rolls and fills. | 17 |
| **Dub** | Echo and reverb used as part of the music. | 19 |
| **Synthwave** | Slow, wide sounds that fade in and wobble. | 21, 22 |
| **Video-game music** | Arpeggios. | 22 |
| **Dance music** | Verse and chorus, build-ups and drops. | 24 to 27 |
| **Hip-hop again** | Swing, and soft and hard hits. | 28 |

SLOOP already has drum kits named for most of these (HOUSE, BOOMBAP, DISCO, LO-FI, TRAP, SYNTHWV, CHIP), so
each song starts from the right sounds.

**About ten songs in all.** Claude drafts each one as a SLOOP 3 project. The author listens and approves it
by ear before any lesson uses it.

**For her voyages, she picks the style.**

**Nothing under copyright goes into this repository,** not even as a test song.

**Each song is kept as a SLOOP 3 project, plus a list of who plays what.** That way Claude and the web page both know
which parts are hers in a given lesson and which to bring in as the band.

## 9. Learning from every play-through

The course gets better by being played. Three things are collected, all on the computer and nowhere else.

- **The logbook.** For each learner, it records each lesson played, how long every step took, and every
  wrong press and every call for help. A step where the author sat for forty seconds is a step
  that needs rewriting.
- **Two questions when a lesson ends,** answered with the OCT buttons: `TOO EASY` or `TOO HARD`, and
  `PLAY IT AGAIN` or `NEXT ONE`.
- **Notes in plain words.** After a session the author tells Claude what felt wrong. Claude reads the
  logbook with those notes and proposes changes to the lesson files.

**What Claude tried goes into the lesson.** Each time Claude leaves the standard path, the logbook keeps
what it did and whether it worked. The explanations that work are written into the lesson as hints, where
the web page version can use them.

**Every lesson is also tested with no person at all.** SLOOP comes with a simulated FM-1 that runs the real
firmware on the computer. A test plays each lesson's standard path on it from start to finish, pressing
what a learner would press, and checks the result. When SLOOP 3 changes a button, the lesson that breaks is found the same
day.

**The sea chest.** The FM-1 holds one project at a time. Before a lesson loads its song, her own project is
copied to the computer, and it is put back when the lesson ends. Her finished songs are kept
there too.

## 10. What has to be built

| Piece | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The guide** | The one addition to the firmware. | It draws the card and the strip, lights any button or key at four levels of brightness or blinking, reports every press and turn and which screen is open, and lets the OCT buttons and keys answer questions. About 1 to 2 KB of the 8 KB that are free. |
| **The connector** | The program that joins Claude to the FM-1. | It talks to the FM-1 over the USB cable, and to the simulated FM-1 the same way. SLOOP's own editor commands already do most of the work: load a project, read and write a pattern, choose a sound, set the mix, press any button. |
| **The lesson files** | One text file a lesson. | Section 5 has a sketch. |
| **The songs** | A SLOOP 3 project each. | Section 8. |
| **The logbook and the sea chest** | Folders on the computer. | Section 9. Kept out of the public repository. |
| **The web page** | The page that plays the lessons with no AI. | It reads the same lesson files and uses the same cable link as SLOOP's editor and installer, which FM-1 owners already know. |

## 11. The order of work

Each step ends with something to play.

1. **First light, led by Claude.** The guide, the connector and lesson 1, all running on the simulated
   FM-1. Then BOATSWAIN goes on the real FM-1, and lesson 1 is played for real. Putting it on the FM-1 is
   the owner's own step, with a full backup first, and only when he says so.
2. **Greenhand, led by Claude.** Lessons 2 to 7 and their song. The author plays the rank, and it is
   revised from his logbook and notes.
3. **The web page, with Greenhand.** Rank 1 playable with no AI. This comes early on purpose: it proves that
   both ways work from the same files while the form of a lesson is still cheap to change.
4. **Deckhand,** then **Able hand,** then **Boatswain.** Each is built, played with Claude, revised, and
   checked on the web page.
5. **The first young learner.** A ten-year-old plays the course from lesson 1 with Claude. The lessons
   change again from what her logbook shows.
6. **Going public, both ways at once.** The public repository, the installer and the lessons web page.
   Then showing it to SLOOP's developer, and offering the guide to SLOOP itself.

## 12. Things to watch

- **SLOOP 3 is a beta and changes often.** Lessons name what to do ("open the drum grid") as well as which
  button does it, and all of them are re-tested on the simulated FM-1 after each new SLOOP.
- **Claude's thinking time.** A few seconds between moves is fine while music plays, and bad on a silent
  screen. Moves are sized so that a wait always falls where the loop is running.
- **The two ways drifting apart.** The four rules in section 5 are there to stop the web page version falling
  behind the Claude-led one.
- **Space in the firmware is tight.** The guide has to stay small. If SLOOP grows, parts the course doesn't
  need can be left out to make room.
- **The FM-1's small speaker can't play low notes.** Lesson 1 turns SLOOP's SPEAKER setting on, or asks for
  headphones.
- **The screen has capital letters only.** All lesson text is written for that from the start.

## 13. Left for later

These were considered and set aside to keep the first version simple. Each has Claude making new material
on the spot, with nothing checked beforehand.

- **A band written to fit her own beat.**
- **Lessons made on request,** such as "show me how to make a spooky beat".
- **Extra practice made up** from what she found hard.

## 14. Questions to settle before building

1. **Is the computer screen part of the lesson?** The plan assumes no: everything she needs is on the FM-1,
   and the computer is there for questions to Claude, typed or spoken.
2. **Does she already know the names of the notes, or play the piano?** That sets the pace of rank 2.
3. **Which kinds of music does she like most?** The songs can lean that way.
4. **Is thirty lessons in four ranks the right size?**
